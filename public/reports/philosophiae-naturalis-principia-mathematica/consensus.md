## 4. Consensus analysis

### A. Operational Order rationale

The seven evaluations converge on the same operative description. The *Principia* is classified at G because its central achievement is not merely mathematical sophistication, improved prediction, representation, or synthesis. The reports repeatedly identify a relocation of explanation: terrestrial and celestial motions cease to be treated as explanatorily separate or merely descriptive regularities and become consequences of a common dynamical architecture involving laws of motion, mathematically characterized forces, and universal gravitation.  

The evaluators also independently converge on the protocol's firewalls. The target—physical motion and dynamical interactions—is independently identifiable; the structure generates consequences concerning independently observable phenomena rather than only within a formal system; mathematical procedure is instrumental rather than the entire basis of the G judgment; and Levels 1–3 do not adequately describe the dominant explanatory operation. 

The Ω increment is supported because the reports find independent successor evidence that the transformed explanatory problem became a non-optional baseline. Later mechanics may revise Newtonian assumptions or replace Newton's ontology, but successors must still preserve, delimit, explain, or supersede the dynamical relations at issue rather than simply restore the prior explanatory landscape. 

### B. Boundary analysis

**Why G is satisfied:** all seven reports identify explanatory relocation rather than merely superior calculation. The common structure does explanatory work across independently constrained terrestrial and celestial phenomena, making lower-order descriptive organization insufficient.

**Why GΩ rather than G:** the reports unanimously conclude that the relocation did not remain merely one optional explanatory architecture. Actual successor inquiry had to reckon with the transformed dynamical problem, even when later theories substantially reformulated or superseded Newtonian commitments. 

**Why not M:** this is likewise highly consistent across the evaluators. The reports distinguish successor-baseline necessity from a constitutive constraint on the entire space of admissible frameworks. They find no demonstration that every adequate physical framework must retain Newtonian absolute space and time, Newtonian force ontology, Newton's particular laws, or inverse-square gravitation as constitutive features. Alternative frameworks can replace those structures while still confronting the transformed explanatory problem. Thus the artifact establishes an unavoidable successor problem, not an unavoidable framework-space constitution.  

The material minority qualification lies at the **G → GΩ gate**: some reports describe the Ω judgment as less secure than G because it depends on how strictly “successor-baseline necessity” is construed and how tightly the relevant successor domain is bounded. No evaluator, however, ultimately assigns G instead of GΩ.

### C. Six-dimensional synthesis

**RL — Relational Load: 9.2 consensus; observed range 9.0–9.3.** The evaluations consistently describe a highly interdependent architecture coordinating foundational dynamical laws, force relations, mathematical structures, orbital mechanics, gravitation, observations, and heterogeneous physical applications. The recurring limiting argument is that much of this architecture remains hierarchically or propositionally decomposable, preventing movement toward maximal interdependence. Disagreement is small: RL has the narrowest range of all six dimensions.  

**AD — Abstraction Depth: 9.2 consensus; observed range 8.7–9.4.** The reports agree that the artifact moves from concrete motions to generalized mathematical and dynamical relations and back to heterogeneous empirical systems while preserving disciplined structural correspondence. The main disagreement is whether this fully clears the 9-level abstraction threshold or remains somewhat below it because the architecture stays within a comparatively coherent physical-mechanical ontology. That produces one of the joint-largest dimensional ranges. 

**CD — Constraint Density: 9.3 consensus; observed range 9.0–9.5.** Evaluators repeatedly emphasize simultaneous mathematical, dynamical, observational, geometrical, and cross-system requirements, with alterations to central assumptions propagating into multiple downstream consequences. The difference among reports is mostly degree: all place CD at or above 9.0, while some see particularly strong convergence of heterogeneous constraints and move toward 9.5. 

**ID — Inferential Distance: 9.4 consensus; observed range 9.0–9.6.** The recurring rationale is the non-routine reconstruction of apparently disparate motions through a unified dynamical architecture, including the bidirectional movement from phenomena to forces and from forces to further phenomena. Evaluators consistently distinguish this from merely executing long difficult calculations. The disagreement concerns how far beyond the 9 anchor the reconstruction should move given that substantial portions become tractable once the central architecture is available. 

**IC — Integrative Compression: 9.4 consensus; observed range 9.0–9.7.** This is one of the strongest and most consistently emphasized dimensions. The reports converge on a relatively compact governing architecture capable of organizing a very large heterogeneous body of physical phenomena while retaining enough structure for detailed derivation or reconstruction. The spread concerns the remaining auxiliary machinery: some evaluators regard the ratio of general principles to recoverable phenomena as exceptionally close to the upper end, whereas others place more weight on the extensive geometry, specialized propositions, and case-specific reasoning still required. 

**GR — Generalization Reach: 9.0 consensus; observed range 8.8–9.4.** All seven reports acknowledge extraordinary transfer across terrestrial and celestial cases, different scales, trajectories, bodies, and gravitational systems. The recurring disagreement is unusually explicit: does enormous variation *within physical mechanics* constitute full GR9-level structural distance, or should the protocol reserve higher scores for transfer across more radically different problem-spaces or ontologies? Three evaluators score 8.8 on precisely this ground, while others judge the internal physical diversity sufficient for 9+. This dimension has the highest standard deviation.

## 6. Evaluator agreement

**TORC agreement: High.** All seven evaluators independently assign GΩ. There is no classification split.

**CMS stability: Moderate.** Six evaluations fall between 920 and 945, but the full observed range is 888–945, producing a 57-point range and population SD of 17.3. The center is quite stable, but the lower evaluation is substantial enough that “High” would overstate numerical convergence.

**Dimensional stability: Moderate.** RL is exceptionally stable, while AD and IC each span 0.7 points and GR has the largest standard deviation. Most of the dimensional disagreement concerns how strictly to treat upper-end anchor requirements, particularly whether very broad generalization within one physical-mechanical ontology warrants GR9+ and how much residual auxiliary structure limits IC and AD.

The largest substantive disagreement is therefore **not the final Operational Order**, but the interpretation of high-end dimensional anchors—especially **Generalization Reach**—with a secondary qualification concerning how narrowly the G→GΩ successor-baseline criterion should be construed.
