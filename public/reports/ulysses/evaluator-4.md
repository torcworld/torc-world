## Operational Order (TORC): **Level 3c — Integrated Recursive Consciousness**

*Ulysses* is best classified at **3c**, not because of its formal difficulty or canonical status, but because its dominant operation makes the relation among consciousness, representation, language, memory, embodiment, and experienced reality itself an object of sustained investigation, then integrates the resulting fractures into a coherent existential structure without eliminating them.

This follows the protocol's distinction between symbolic and recursive consciousness: Level 3 begins only where symbolic organization is insufficient and the consciousness–reality relation itself becomes the object of inquiry; 3c additionally requires preservation of existential contradiction within a coherent orientation rather than simple resolution. 

Across the completed novel, events are repeatedly reconstituted through different consciousnesses and representational systems. Bloom's ordinary experience, Stephen's intellectual estrangement, Molly's retrospective consciousness, memory, bodily sensation, language, cultural inheritance, sexuality, grief, paternity, mortality, and social identity continually alter one another's intelligibility. Crucially, the changing styles are not merely decorative representations of a stable reality: the artifact repeatedly demonstrates that what counts as experienced reality is inseparable from the forms through which consciousness apprehends and represents it.

The completed structure nevertheless does not terminate in fragmentation. Bloom's return home, the provisional Bloom–Stephen encounter, and especially Molly's final movement integrate contradiction—marital estrangement and attachment, fidelity and infidelity, mortality and vitality, memory and immediacy—without making those contradictions disappear. The resulting coherence is plural and unstable rather than reconciliatory. That satisfies the protocol's defining distinction between **integration** and mere closure or consolation. 

### Boundary analysis

**Why 3c rather than 3b.** A 3b reading captures much of the novel: consciousness confronts alienation, mortality, failed communication, unstable identity, epistemic uncertainty, and the inadequacy of stable representational viewpoints. But 3b requires the dominant operation to remain confrontation rather than integration.  *Ulysses* goes further. Its proliferating perspectives ultimately coexist within a larger architecture of ordinary embodied life. Bloom, Stephen, and Molly do not achieve philosophical resolution, yet their incompatible orientations become mutually contextualizing components of the completed whole. The novel therefore preserves fracture while constructing a viable orientation capable of containing it—the positive operation required for 3c.

The **Integration Resistance Principle** is satisfied: the classification does not depend on the ending being hopeful, reconciliatory, or emotionally affirmative. The contradictions remain operative. Bloom's marriage is not repaired into simplicity; Stephen's alienation is not cured; mortality, sexual jealousy, isolation, contingency, and representational instability remain. What changes is their place inside the completed architecture. That is integration rather than elimination. 

**Why not G.** This is the decisive upper boundary. The protocol explicitly forbids inferring G from complexity, recursion, abstraction, novelty, or formal virtuosity. G requires **explanatory relocation** of an independently identifiable target: the artifact must disclose a generative structure that explains why otherwise distinct target phenomena take the forms they do, rather than principally arranging them within a new picture. 

A plausible G candidate would say that *Ulysses* relocates the explanation of human experience into representational consciousness or language. But under the protocol's strict tests, that claim does not clear the gate. Much of the apparent generativity is produced through the novel's own representational machinery—shifting styles, interior monologue, linguistic transformations, correspondences, and perspectival reconstruction. The protocol's **Artifact-Internal Generativity Firewall** specifically says that novel semantic or representational rules do not establish G when they primarily explain how the artifact constructs its own meanings. An independently identifiable explanatory target must instead have its explanatory foundation relocated. 

Thus:

- **Independent target:** human consciousness/experienced reality can be stated independently of the novel. 
- **Candidate mechanism:** experience becomes intelligible through plural, embodied, linguistically mediated consciousness. 
- **Generative-mechanism test:** **not sufficiently established.** The architecture exceptionally represents and recursively investigates that mediation, but does not establish a generative mechanism explaining independently constrained instances of consciousness in the protocol's stronger sense. 
- **Explanatory replacement:** **fails.** A recursive/integrative account remains sufficient to explain the novel's dominant operation. 
- **Explanatory-object firewall:** the enormous transformation of narrative representation does not itself establish transformation of the explanatory foundation of the independent target. 
- **Levels 1–3 insufficiency test:** **fails for G**, because 3c already supplies a sufficient account. 

Accordingly, the lowest sufficient classification is **3c**. This also follows the protocol's instruction that higher-order appearance cannot substitute for demonstrated operation.
