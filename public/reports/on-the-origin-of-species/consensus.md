## 4. Consensus analysis

### A. Operational Order rationale

Across all seven reports, the stable lower-bound finding is **G**. The recurring argument is that the artifact's dominant operation is a generative explanatory relocation rather than representation, classification, symbolic organization, or recursive integration. Evaluators repeatedly identify an interacting architecture of variation, inheritance, differential preservation or reproduction, divergence, extinction, and common descent, used to make otherwise separate biological phenomena mutually intelligible. One report describes the relevant target as patterned diversity, adaptation, differentiation, extinction, geographical distribution, and hierarchical affinities, and stresses that the mechanism changes what explains those phenomena rather than merely reorganizing their description.

The consensus rises from G to **GΩ** because six evaluators conclude that the explanatory relocation became a non-optional baseline for adequate successor inquiry. Their common position is carefully narrower than saying later biology must preserve every Darwinian proposition. Rather, successor frameworks can revise, supplement, or reject components of the original mechanism while still being unable to return intact to the previous explanatory organization. The reports therefore locate Ω at the level of the **transformed explanatory problem-space**, not at the level of doctrinal fidelity to Darwin.

The reports also converge that **M is not established**. The recurrent distinction is that successor-baseline necessity is not equivalent to demonstrating a constitutive constraint on the entire space of admissible frameworks. The evaluations describe *Origin* as relocating biological explanation without establishing the stronger framework-space necessity required for M.

### B. Boundary analysis

**Why the artifact satisfies GΩ:** The reports unanimously find the G gate satisfied: an independently identifiable biological target is addressed through a generative causal structure that produces explanatory consequences across distinct phenomena and makes lower-order descriptions inadequate. Six evaluations additionally find sufficient successor evidence that the relocated evolutionary problem cannot simply be ignored by adequate later inquiry.

**Why it does not satisfy M:** The reports do not find evidence that *Origin* establishes an inescapable constitutive constraint on every admissible biological explanatory framework. Its particular causal mechanisms remain revisable, supplementable, and partially replaceable. This is compatible with GΩ because the transformed problem-space can remain mandatory without Darwin's specific solution being mandatory.

**Material disagreement at G/GΩ:** The minority evaluator regards the Ω criterion as unavailable under an artifact-only evidential restriction and therefore stops at G. The GΩ majority instead treats external successor evidence as specifically required and admissible for the Ω gate. Even among the majority, several reports flag attribution as the principal residual uncertainty: whether the mandatory successor baseline belongs specifically enough to *Origin*'s relocation rather than to the later reconstructed evolutionary synthesis.

**AD — Abstraction Depth**

**Consensus: 8.8**

**CD — Constraint Density**

**Consensus: 8.7**

The disagreement concerns how narrowly these constraints restrict the viable construction space. Some evaluators emphasize the replaceability of subsidiary mechanisms and therefore remain in the mid-to-high 8s; one finds the coupled requirements sufficient for 9.0.

**ID — Inferential Distance**

**Consensus: 9.0**
**Evaluator range: 9.0–9.1**

This is the most stable dimension. Every evaluator places ID at 9.0 or 9.1. The common rationale is that dispersed observations concerning variation, selection, competition, geography, fossils, affinities, and related phenomena do not routinely entail the successful historical-generative synthesis. The reports therefore converge on exceptional inferential reconstruction with virtually no material disagreement.

**IC — Integrative Compression**

**Consensus: 9.1**
**Evaluator range: 9.0–9.3**

Evaluators repeatedly identify this as one of the artifact's strongest capacities: a relatively compact architecture organizes a very large and heterogeneous biological evidential field while retaining enough structure to reconstruct distinct phenomena rather than merely summarizing them. Evaluator 1 explicitly calls integrative compression a leading strength, while other reports reach as high as 9.3. The small spread concerns the extent to which auxiliary hypotheses limit movement beyond the 9 anchor.

**GR — Generalization Reach**

**Consensus: 8.4**
**Evaluator range: 8.2–9.0**

GR produces the largest dimensional spread. Most reports distinguish enormous transfer across biological taxa, environments, evidence classes, and timescales from transfer across genuinely distant problem-spaces or ontologies. This domain-distance restriction explains scores in the low-to-high 8s. Evaluator 3 instead assigns 9.0, producing the upper endpoint of the range. The disagreement is therefore not about whether the structure generalizes broadly, but about how the protocol should weight very broad **within-domain** transfer relative to structurally distant cross-domain transfer.

# Consensus Evaluation

Across seven independent TORC/DIP evaluations, *On the Origin of Species* receives a consensus classification of **GΩ — Generative Consciousness: Successor-Baseline Necessity**, with a median **CMS of 890** and a mean of **886.7**. Six evaluators assign GΩ and one assigns G. The disagreement is narrow and protocol-specific: all seven identify the artifact as clearing the G threshold through a generative explanatory relocation; the sole dissent stops at G because it treats the external successor evidence required for Ω as unavailable under its artifact-only evidential interpretation.

The common G-level finding is unusually stable. Across the reports, the artifact is characterized as coordinating variation, inheritance, competition, differential survival or reproduction, accumulation, divergence, extinction, and common descent into an architecture that makes multiple independently identifiable biological phenomena mutually intelligible. Its achievement is therefore not described merely as greater descriptive scope or theoretical sophistication. The recurring judgment is that it changes what performs the explanatory work: adaptation, differentiation, affinities, distribution, extinction, and related biological patterns are reorganized as consequences of historical and population-level processes.

The six-evaluator GΩ majority further concludes that this relocation became a non-optional baseline for adequate successor inquiry. Their claim is not that later biology must preserve Darwin's exact mechanisms. On the contrary, the reports explicitly acknowledge substantial revision, supplementation, and replacement of particular components. The Ω judgment rests instead on the proposition that successor evolutionary inquiry cannot simply restore the earlier explanatory landscape while remaining adequate to the same target. The strongest residual uncertainty is whether that necessity should be attributed specifically to *Origin*'s achieved relocation or more broadly to the evolutionary framework subsequently reconstructed through genetics and later biological theory.
The dimensional profile is correspondingly high but not uniform. **Inferential Distance** is the most stable dimension at a median 9.0 and a range of only 9.0–9.1, reflecting near-unanimous agreement on the non-routine reconstruction required to connect heterogeneous observations into the successful explanatory architecture. **Integrative Compression** is another major strength at 9.1, reflecting the ability of a compact causal structure to organize an unusually large evidential field. **Relational Load** likewise reaches 9.1 because the reports find genuine mutual constraint among multiple biological scales and evidence classes rather than mere component count.

The comparatively lower medians in **Abstraction Depth (8.8)** and **Constraint Density (8.7)** reflect bounded reservations: the architecture remains principally a biological domain theory rather than an unrestricted meta-representational system, and meaningful flexibility remains in subsidiary mechanisms and local explanations. **Generalization Reach (8.4)** is both the lowest consensus dimension and the most disputed. Most evaluators regard the artifact's enormous breadth across biological cases as insufficient by itself to demonstrate 9-level transfer across structurally distant problem-spaces, while one evaluator scores GR at 9.0.

The resulting profile is therefore both strong and coherent: **GΩ | 890**. The consensus identifies a generative explanatory relocation of exceptional inferential and integrative magnitude, with successor-baseline necessity supported by a clear evaluator majority but carrying a real and explicitly preserved G/GΩ boundary dispute. The seven evaluations do not support promotion to M, because none establishes the stronger constitutive constraint on admissible framework-space required at that level.
