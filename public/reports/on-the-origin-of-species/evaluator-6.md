## Operational Order (TORC): **GΩ — Generative Consciousness: Successor-Baseline Necessity**

Treating *On the Origin of Species* strictly as the artifact to be evaluated, rather than awarding anything for Darwin's reputation or the book's canonical status, I classify it at **GΩ**.

The protocol makes the crucial distinction that **G relocates explanation**, whereas **GΩ makes that relocation an unavoidable baseline for adequate successor inquiry**; mere complexity, originality, importance, or influence cannot establish either. 

The dominant operation of *Origin* is not simply the accumulation of biological observations or the symbolic/recursive interpretation of them. It proposes a compact generative structure—**descent with modification operating through variation, inheritance, differential survival/reproduction, divergence, and long accumulation of small changes**—as an explanation for a large collection of independently identifiable biological phenomena: adaptation, species divergence, taxonomic hierarchy, geographical distribution, homology, rudimentary organs, succession in the fossil record, and the patterned unity and diversity of organisms.

That structure does genuine explanatory work. The phenomena are not merely placed inside a new picture: common descent plus branching modification and selection generate expectations about why organisms should exhibit nested affinities, why related forms should cluster geographically, why adaptation should be imperfect and historically constrained, why rudimentary structures should persist, and why classification should have genealogical structure. That is the kind of distinction the protocol requires between an *explanatory schema* and a *generative explanatory mechanism*. 

### Boundary analysis

**Why GΩ rather than 3c.** Level 3c would require recursive integration of contradiction into a coherent existential orientation. *Origin* certainly integrates anomalies and apparent contradictions—extinction, imperfect adaptation, sterile castes, difficult cases for selection—but that is not its dominant operation. Its achievement concerns the explanatory basis of an independently existing target, biological diversity. A Level-3 description therefore leaves the central scientific operation unexplained. Under the protocol, G begins exactly where an artifact changes the explanatory foundation through which such a target becomes intelligible. 

**Explicit G gate.** The independent target is **the diversification, adaptation, affinities, distribution, and historical succession of living forms**. The candidate structure is evolutionary descent with modification, centrally driven in the artifact by natural selection. It generates consequences across distinct instances of that target rather than merely arranging observations. It changes *what explains* biological form and diversity rather than merely improving a method for describing or calculating them, so the explanatory-object and computational/procedural firewalls are passed. It is also not artifact-internal generativity: organisms and their empirical relations constrain the theory independently of the book's vocabulary. Finally, lower representational, symbolic, and recursive accounts are insufficient to characterize what the completed theory actually does. These satisfy the protocol's G requirements: previously unseen generative structure, explanatory relocation, successful domain organization, and insufficiency of Levels 1–3.  

**Why GΩ rather than merely G.** Here the protocol permits—and requires—external historical evidence, but only for one narrowly defined purpose: establishing *successor-baseline necessity*, not rewarding influence or fame. GΩ requires that adequate successor inquiry cannot simply ignore the relocation and return to the preceding explanatory landscape.  Modern biological inquiry can reject Darwin's particular mechanisms, alter their relative importance, incorporate genetics and molecular biology, distinguish selection from drift, and substantially revise Darwin's specific empirical claims. But an adequate account of adaptation, speciation, phylogeny, biogeography, comparative anatomy, and biological diversity cannot simply restore the pre-evolutionary explanatory organization as though common descent and population-level historical transformation had never become explanatory problems. Successor theories must reckon with that transformed problem-space. This is structural successor necessity rather than mere citation or reverence, satisfying the protocol's Ω gate. 

**Why not M.** *Origin* does **not** establish a constitutive constraint upon the entire space of admissible biological explanatory frameworks. Its evolutionary architecture may be an unavoidable successor baseline, but the book does not prove that every adequate framework of the relevant class must obey some formally or conceptually inescapable constraint such that attempts to evade it reproduce an impossibility or contradiction. That stronger property is precisely what separates M from GΩ.  An alternative evolutionary mechanism can replace major parts of Darwin's particular causal framework while remaining coherent. Thus the Framework-Space Test fails: the work transforms the explanatory landscape but does not delimit all admissible framework-space in the protocol's constitutive sense.
