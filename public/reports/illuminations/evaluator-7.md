## Operational Order (TORC): **Level 3b — Fractured Recursive Consciousness**

I am treating the artifact as the completed poetic collection *Illuminations*, rather than using Rimbaud's reputation, biography, influence, or canonical status as evidence. That follows the protocol's artifact-first and anti-prestige requirements. 

The collection's dominant operation is best classified as **fractured recursive consciousness**. Its symbolic transformations are extraordinarily dense, but they do more than encode meanings about represented objects. Across the collection, perception, language, identity, value, urban reality, memory, desire, and the relation between consciousness and world repeatedly cease to function as stable representational coordinates. The poems do not merely give strange symbolic pictures of reality; they repeatedly make the *conditions under which reality can be coherently apprehended* unstable.

That is the decisive Level-3 move under TORC: representation/orientation itself becomes implicated in what is being investigated, rather than functioning simply as the vehicle for symbolic meaning.

At the same time, *Illuminations* does not globally construct a stable higher orientation capable of containing this fracture. Its characteristic achievement is closer to sustained **confrontation, multiplication, destabilization, and reconstruction of consciousness/world relations** than to their integration. Hence 3b rather than 3c.

### Boundary analysis

**Why 3b rather than Level 2.** Level 2 would be sufficient if the collection's dominant achievement could be exhausted by symbolic organization: images such as cities, machines, bodies, landscapes, light, architecture, childhood, commerce, royalty, catastrophe, and metamorphosis standing in patterned relations and generating meanings beyond themselves. The protocol explicitly warns that symbolism, ambiguity, abstraction, fragmentation, surrealism, and unconventional structure are only signals; the relevant question is what operation they actually perform. 

Here symbolic organization does not quite exhaust the work. The instability operates recursively upon the act of intelligibility itself. Spatial scales collapse into one another; perceiver and perceived exchange functions; ordinary causal and categorical boundaries are repeatedly suspended; voices and perspectives cease to provide reliable orientation; concrete objects become simultaneously perceptual events, conceptual relations, and transformations of consciousness. The poems' difficulty is therefore not merely that their symbols require interpretation. They repeatedly deprive interpretation of a single stable representational level from which to operate.

This also survives the protocol's resistance test in the relevant sense. Removing the collection's unusual imagery would certainly destroy much of its poetic mechanism, but the disclosed fracture is not simply "there are bizarre things in this invented poetic world." The underlying operation concerns consciousness encountering a world that cannot be stabilized into ordinary perceptual, semantic, existential, or categorical relations. Symbolic organization is therefore necessary but not sufficient.

**Why 3b rather than 3a.** The dominant fracture is not primarily corrective. Individual poems certainly contain social, economic, religious, political, and civilizational pressures, but the collection does not principally diagnose an external organization of reality and orient itself toward repairing it. Its deeper instability concerns the relation of consciousness, language, perception, and existence themselves.

**Why not 3c.** The protocol requires genuine integration of the existential contradiction into a coherent orientation while preserving the contradiction. Mere beauty, revelation, transcendence, reconciliation, spiritual imagery, or closure is insufficient. The resulting structure must actually contain the fracture coherently. That is not the dominant global operation of *Illuminations*. Its moments of visionary synthesis remain unstable, local, or themselves subject to further transformation. The collection more characteristically **inhabits fracture than integrates it**.

**Why not G.** The apparent candidate would be a poetic structure in which ordinary subject/object, language/world, and perception/reality relations are replaced by a new generative model of experience. But this fails TORC's G threshold. The protocol requires an independently identifiable explanatory target and a structure that *explains why otherwise distinct target phenomena take the forms they do*, rather than principally arranging them within a newly conceived picture. 

*Illuminations* creates remarkably powerful new configurations of experience, but it does not establish a generative explanatory mechanism for an independently identifiable target domain such that Levels 1–3 become inadequate. Its transformations remain principally poetic/representational and recursive rather than explanatory relocation. Calling the collection G because it radically reinvents poetic consciousness would therefore confuse artifact-internal generativity with TORC generativity.

The lowest sufficient classification is consequently **3b**. This is important because TORC explicitly requires uncertainty to resolve downward and forbids treating visible complexity or apparent generativity as operational achievement.
