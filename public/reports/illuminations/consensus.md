## 4. Consensus analysis

### A. Operational Order rationale

The consensus classification is **Level 3b — Fractured Recursive Consciousness**.

All seven evaluators agree that direct representation is radically insufficient and that symbolic transformation is pervasive. Cities, bodies, landscapes, machinery, social structures, commerce, religion, technology, sensory experience, and identity are repeatedly reorganized through cross-domain relations. The disagreement begins only after Level 2 has unquestionably been established.

The four-report majority finds an additional globally recurrent operation: these transformations do not merely create symbolic meanings but destabilize the standpoint from which meaning, perception, identity, and reality can be organized at all. In their shared formulation, consciousness does not simply interpret an unusual world; its own relation to world, language, selfhood, perception, and intelligibility becomes unstable and recursively implicated. That is the evidence supporting the move from symbolic consciousness to fractured recursive consciousness.  

Within Level 3, the reports consistently reject **3a** because correction of an external social, political, moral, or institutional fracture is not the governing movement. They reject **3c** because moments of visionary synthesis or affirmation never become a stable global architecture capable of preserving and integrating the contradiction. The 3b reports describe the dominant motion instead as confrontation, rupture, metamorphosis, replacement, and renewed destabilization.  

### B. Boundary analysis

**Why the consensus satisfies 3b.** The majority finds symbolism necessary but not sufficient. Repeated transformations affect not just represented objects but subject-position, viewpoint, categorical stability, representation, and the conditions under which a coherent world can appear. The same recursive pressure appears across materially different kinds of poems, which the majority treats as evidence of global recurrence rather than an isolated local effect. 

**Why not 3c.** None of the seven reports establishes dominant global integration. The strongest 3b reports explicitly acknowledge local synthesis, affirmation, transfiguration, or apparent reconciliation, but hold that these moments remain local or unstable. The collection does not globally contain its fractures within a coherent existential orientation while preserving them.  

**Minority objection.** Three evaluators stop at Level 2 because they regard the very devices producing the apparent existential instability—symbolism, metaphorical recombination, syntactic discontinuity, perceptual mutation—as sufficient explanations of it. The resulting consensus is therefore real but narrow: 3b wins by four evaluations to three, not by elimination of the Level-2 interpretation.
