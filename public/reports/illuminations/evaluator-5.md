## TORC classification: **Level 3b — Fractured Recursive Consciousness**

*Illuminations*, treated strictly as the completed artifact and without using Rimbaud’s reputation, canonical standing, or historical influence as evidence, is best classified at **3b**.

The protocol requires the evaluator to classify what the artifact *does*, rather than rewarding profundity, symbolism, abstraction, difficulty, or prestige; it also requires the lowest classification sufficient to explain the dominant global operation. 

The collection clearly exceeds direct representation. Its dominant procedure repeatedly converts represented reality into unstable symbolic configurations: city, body, commodity, spectacle, childhood, technology, landscape, violence, erotic experience, language, and perception continually become mutually transforming representational fields. But **Level 2 is not sufficient**. The recurrent instability is not merely a symbolic code pointing toward a second layer of meaning. Representation and orientation themselves repeatedly become problematic: perception produces worlds whose status cannot be stabilized; consciousness encounters reality through incompatible orders; identity and viewpoint mutate with what is perceived.

That is the protocol's Level-3 threshold: symbolic organization ceases to suffice when the relationship among consciousness, representation, interpretation, and reality becomes the object of the operation. 

More specifically, **3b rather than 3a** is warranted because the collection does not primarily locate its fracture in a corrigible social, political, institutional, or moral order. Its recurrent contradiction is more fundamental: consciousness cannot settle into a stable relation with world, self, language, sensation, or meaning. The protocol defines 3b precisely by such an irreducible contradiction between consciousness and existence, whose dominant movement is confrontation rather than correction. 

### Boundary analysis

**Why 3b rather than 3a:** 3a would require the dominant fracture to reside in external reality and to orient the recursive operation toward correction. *Illuminations* certainly contains social and civilizational material, but this does not organize the collection globally. Its cities, crowds, economies, bodies, machines, landscapes, and speakers participate in a wider destabilization of intelligibility itself. The fracture persists even where no social correction is at issue. Thus the existential-resistance requirement is met: removing the stylistic strangeness would not remove the underlying problem of consciousness attempting—and failing—to establish a stable reconciliation with existence. The protocol specifically distinguishes such disclosure from mere surrealism, dream logic, ambiguity, or psychological instability. 

**Why not 3c:** *Illuminations* does not globally perform the additional operation required by integrated recursive consciousness. It can produce moments of visionary synthesis, totalizing perception, or apparent arrival, but the collection does not establish a durable coherent existential orientation capable of **containing the contradiction while preserving it**. The dominant motion remains metamorphosis, confrontation, rupture, replacement, and renewed destabilization. Under the protocol, hope, spiritual vision, reconciliation, or closure are insufficient: 3c requires genuine structural integration of the preserved existential contradiction.  

Accordingly, there is no need to invoke G as the assigned level. Indeed, the collection's extraordinary symbolic and representational inventiveness should not be mistaken for explanatory relocation: the protocol explicitly prohibits treating complexity, abstraction, originality, or artifact-internal generativity as sufficient evidence of G. The collection generates radically transformed experiential worlds, but it does not establish a generative explanatory mechanism for an independently identifiable target that renders the previous explanatory basis insufficient. That distinction is decisive.
