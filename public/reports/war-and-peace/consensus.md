## 3. TORC consensus

The evaluator distribution is **3c, 3c, 3c, 3c, 3c, 3c, G**. Thus **3c occurs six times and G once**. The modal classification is therefore **3c**, and the clear-majority **consensus TORC is 3c — Integrated Recursive Consciousness**.

The raw agreement rate is 6/7, or about 86%. That majority should not, however, obscure the importance of the disagreement: the single dissent crosses the protocol's categorical **3c → G** boundary. All seven evaluators identify essentially the same candidate responsible for that boundary question: the novel's reconstruction of **historical causation**, particularly its displacement of great-agent or commander-centered explanation by a model involving innumerable interacting actions, circumstances, constraints, necessity, and distributed causation. The six 3c evaluators regard this as a serious G candidate but conclude that the required generative/explanatory-replacement operation either is insufficiently mechanism-like or is not globally dominant enough to make 3c inadequate.  

Evaluator 7 makes the opposite gate determination. That report treats historical causation as an independently identifiable target and argues that the distributed causal model generates repeated explanatory consequences—such as the divergence of commanders' intentions from outcomes, decentralized battlefield causation, retrospective over-attribution of agency, and changing apparent freedom with scale—and therefore passes both the generative-mechanism and explanatory-replacement tests. 

The disagreement is consequently **not about whether the historical-causation structure exists**. It is about whether that structure constitutes a genuine generative explanatory replacement and, above all, whether it is sufficiently dominant across the completed artifact to render Level 3 explanation insufficient.

## 4. Consensus analysis

### A. Operational Order rationale

The consensus classification of **3c** rests on unusually broad independent agreement about the artifact's dominant completed operation. The six majority reports characterize *War and Peace* as repeatedly making the relation between consciousness and reality itself an object of inquiry: intention confronts historical necessity, explanatory schemes confront lived experience, heroic agency confronts causal impotence, and meaningful existence confronts mortality, suffering, contingency, and limited knowledge.  

Crucially, those reports do not describe the work as terminating in fracture. They repeatedly identify a completed movement toward an orientation capable of **containing contradiction without eliminating it**. Pierre, Andrei, Natasha, the domestic trajectories, the war material, and the historical-philosophical structure are repeatedly cited as participating in that integration. This distinction—contradiction remains operative, but consciousness achieves a coherent orientation within it—is the recurring basis for 3c rather than 3b.  

The dissenting G evaluation does not reject this 3c structure; it explicitly calls the 3c reading strong. Its disagreement is instead that 3c allegedly becomes insufficient once the novel's historical explanatory replacement is taken into account. That makes the consensus especially clear about the lower boundary while leaving a genuine but minority challenge at the upper one.

### B. Boundary analysis

**Why 3c is satisfied:** Across the reports, 3b is treated as inadequate because confrontation with mortality, contingency, failed mastery, historical opacity, and the limits of agency does not remain the terminal operation. The artifact moves toward workable forms of participation, relationship, ordinary existence, and limited agency without reversing or denying the experiences that produced the fracture. Evaluators repeatedly emphasize that this is not merely optimistic closure: suffering, death, necessity, and epistemic limitation remain structurally active. 

**Why the consensus does not rise to G:** The six-report majority accepts historical causation as an independently identifiable candidate target but denies that the higher gate is fully established. Their recurring reasons are twofold. First, the distributed-causation account can be understood as an unusually powerful critique, reconceptualization, or explanatory schema rather than a sufficiently determinate generative mechanism. Second, and even more consistently, it does not replace 3c as the **lowest sufficient explanation of the completed artifact**: mortality, love, family, spiritual transformation, suffering, forgiveness, and the movement from existential fracture to integration remain centrally intelligible through the recursive 3c architecture. 

**Minority G argument:** Evaluator 7 accepts precisely the gates the majority rejects. It argues that the distributed causal structure generates recurring explanatory consequences across an independent target and that the prior great-agent model becomes positively inadequate rather than merely supplemented. On that view, a large global part of the novel—battles, command, Napoleon/Kutuzov, Moscow, the retreat, and the epilogue—cannot be sufficiently accounted for by existential integration alone. The disagreement therefore turns on **generative-mechanism status and global explanatory dominance**, not on a different reading of the basic evidence.

## 6. Evaluator agreement

**TORC agreement: Moderate.** Numerically, agreement is strong at six of seven evaluators, but the dissent crosses the important 3c→G categorical boundary. Because that disagreement concerns the protocol's explanatory-relocation gate rather than a minor subclassification, “High” would understate its substantive importance.

**CMS stability: High.** The median is 838, the mean 834.6, and the population SD is 13.70 on an approximately 835-point central score. Six evaluations lie between 807 and 842, with the G evaluator at 855. The spread changes the precise magnitude estimate but not the overall conclusion that the artifact scores very highly.

**Dimensional stability: Moderate.** RL and AD are exceptionally stable, with ranges of only 0.3 and 0.2. The largest disagreement occurs in **CD (range 1.0)**, followed by **ID (0.9)** and **IC (0.8)**. According to the reports, this reflects differing judgments about how narrowly the artifact's successful construction space is constrained, how non-routine and underdetermined its central inferential restructurings are, and how much of its immense integration constitutes genuine compression rather than explicitly retained complexity.
