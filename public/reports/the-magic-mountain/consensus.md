## 3. TORC consensus

The seven TORC classifications are:

**3b, 3b, 3b, 3c, 3c, 3c, 3b**

Frequency:

- **3b — 4/7 evaluators (57.1%)**
- **3c — 3/7 evaluators (42.9%)**

The **modal classification is 3b**, and because four of seven evaluators classify the artifact at 3b, the consensus TORC is **Level 3b — Fractured Recursive Consciousness**.

The agreement is nevertheless only **Moderate**, not High. This is not diffuse disagreement about the work's operational order. All seven reports agree that symbolic representation alone is insufficient, that the artifact reaches recursive Level 3, and that G is not demonstrated. The disagreement falls almost entirely on the **3b → 3c integration gate**.

The 3b evaluators argue that the “Snow” episode provides genuine local evidence of integration but does not become a stable governing orientation across the completed artifact. They point to Castorp's failure to durably consolidate the insight, the subsequent intensification of the Settembrini–Naphta conflict, Naphta's catastrophic end, the movement into war, and the interrogative rather than achieved quality of the ending. In this reading, **confrontation remains globally dominant**.
The 3c evaluators accept essentially the same instability but interpret its significance differently. For them, the “Snow” insight establishes a coherent humanistic orientation that preserves death, irrationality, and contradiction rather than resolving them. The later catastrophe therefore **tests the integration without cancelling it**; precariousness is not taken to disqualify 3c.
That is the precise operational boundary at issue.

## 4. Consensus analysis

### A. Operational Order rationale

**Consensus TORC: Level 3b — Fractured Recursive Consciousness.**

Across all seven reports, the artifact is understood as exceeding symbolic representation and making the conditions by which consciousness organizes existence into a sustained object of investigation. Recurring evaluator evidence centers on unstable relations among health and illness, life and death, reason and irrationality, eros and mortality, subjective and chronological time, ideological systems, and historical existence. The reports therefore converge strongly that Level 3 is positively demonstrated.
The majority classification is 3b because four evaluators judge **recursive confrontation**, rather than achieved integration, to remain the dominant global operation of the completed artifact. The “Snow” episode is not dismissed; all four majority evaluators treat it as the strongest evidence for 3c. Their shared conclusion is instead that the insight is insufficiently stabilized across what follows to make integration the governing completed operation.

This is a narrow majority rather than a decisive one. Three evaluators regard precisely that same precarious integration as sufficient for 3c.

### B. Boundary analysis

#### Why the artifact satisfies 3b

The evaluators broadly agree that the contradiction is existential rather than merely corrective. The underlying tensions are not reducible to bad institutions, deficient politics, defective morality, or a social order that could simply be repaired. Mortality, embodiment, subjective time, eros, rational limitation, incompatible modes of orientation, and consciousness's need for meaning remain operative beneath the novel's symbolic construction. The 3b evaluators therefore regard the existential-resistance criterion as satisfied.

#### Why consensus does not advance to 3c

The decisive issue is not whether integration ever occurs. Every report identifies the “Snow” episode as important positive evidence. The issue is whether that integration becomes **sufficiently global, coherent, and structurally sustained to govern the completed artifact**.

The 3b majority says no: Castorp's insight does not securely govern what follows, competing orientations continue to collide, the ideological conflict terminates catastrophically, and war reopens rather than contains the existential fracture.

The 3c minority says yes: integration need not amount to permanent psychological retention, resolution, optimism, or closure. On that reading, the humanistic orientation produced through the “Snow” experience remains structurally available precisely because it contains contradiction rather than abolishing it; subsequent catastrophe functions as a stress test.

The consensus therefore remains **3b by a 4–3 majority**, but the boundary should be published as genuinely contested rather than presented as settled.

The next higher major-order boundary, **G**, is substantially less contentious. Evaluators on both sides of the 3b/3c split agree that complexity, philosophical scope, abstraction, recursion, and semantic productivity do not themselves establish a generative operational order. They repeatedly conclude that no independently constrained target receives the kind of explanatory relocation or generative mechanism required for G.

## 5. Six-dimensional synthesis

### RL — Relational Load

**Consensus result: 8.8**
**Mean: 8.87**
**Observed range: 8.7–9.0**

All seven evaluators describe a dense, nonlocal dependency network linking Castorp's development with illness and health, temporality, mortality, eros, ideological interlocutors, medical structures, recurring motifs, and European history. The common distinction between scores in the high 8s and 9.0 concerns how completely this heterogeneous material is mutually interdependent: several evaluators retain some allowance for semi-autonomous episodes or decomposable structures, while the 9.0 scorers regard the cross-domain interdependence as exceptional. The disagreement is small.

### AD — Abstraction Depth

**Consensus result: 8.7**
**Mean: 8.81**
**Observed range: 8.6–9.0**

Evaluators consistently identify multiple coordinated abstraction layers connecting concrete bodily, temporal, erotic, medical, and narrative particulars with mortality, ideology, consciousness, civilization, and history. The main scoring distinction is whether this architecture reaches a sufficiently disciplined meta-level or invariant organization for 9.0. Three evaluators say yes; four place it just below.

### CD — Compression/Constraint Density

**Consensus result: 8.2**
**Mean: 8.17**
**Observed range: 7.8–8.4**

The reports agree that many heterogeneous demands must coexist: character development, ideological confrontation, temporal architecture, recurring motifs, medical or bodily material, historical trajectory, irony, and the Bildungsroman structure. They also repeatedly warn against converting the sheer complexity of a large novel into extreme constraint density. The principal disagreement concerns how much local substitution, expansion, compression, or rearrangement could occur while preserving the central achievement. One evaluator stops at 7.8; the others score 8.1–8.4.

### ID — Inferential Demand

**Consensus result: 8.2**
**Mean: 8.19**
**Observed range: 8.0–8.4**

There is strong agreement that the artifact depends on non-routine conceptual restructurings connecting illness with epistemic instability, subjective time with historical consciousness, pedagogy with ideology, eros with death, and private formation with historical catastrophe. Scores remain clustered because evaluators also agree that dialogue, motif, narrator commentary, and repeated conceptual staging provide meaningful scaffolding, preventing the inferential relations from reaching extreme underdetermination.

### IC — Integration Complexity / Integrative Compression

**Consensus result: 8.7**
**Mean: 8.59**
**Observed range: 8.2–8.8**

All reports identify a comparatively compact set of governing structures—especially mountain/flatland, health/illness, altered time, eros/death, Bildung, and the major ideological figures—as organizing a very large heterogeneous field. The main limiting consideration is also shared: the work achieves this integration partly through deliberate expansiveness and discursive elaboration, so exceptionally high integration does not automatically imply equally exceptional compression economy. IC has one of the widest observed ranges, but the underlying reasoning is highly consistent.

### GR — Generative/Generalization Reach

**Consensus result: 7.4**
**Mean: 7.46**
**Observed range: 7.2–7.8**

This is the lowest dimension in every evaluator's profile. The reports agree that the architecture transfers substantially beyond the literal sanatorium to problems involving mortality, ideological formation, education, institutional enclosure, temporality, and existential orientation. They also agree that broad human relevance must not be mistaken for demonstrated structural transfer across markedly heterogeneous problem-spaces. Differences from 7.2 to 7.8 reflect how close each evaluator believes the architecture comes to the 8-level transfer threshold, not disagreement about the basic limitation.

## 7. Evaluator agreement

**TORC agreement: Moderate**

There is unanimous agreement on Level 3 rather than symbolic-only operation and broad agreement that G is not warranted, but the 4–3 division at the consequential 3b → 3c boundary prevents a High rating.

**CMS stability: High**

All seven CMS values lie between 817 and 848, with a median of 840 and standard deviation of about 11 points on the 1000-point scale. The scores therefore describe substantially the same magnitude profile despite the categorical TORC dispute.

**Dimensional stability: High**

The dimensional evaluations are tightly clustered. RL varies by only 0.3; AD and ID by 0.4; and CD, IC, and GR by 0.6. More importantly, evaluators repeatedly identify the same profile shape: relational load and abstraction depth are strongest, integration is also very high, while GR is the common bottleneck.

The largest substantively important disagreement is therefore **not dimensional scoring but the interpretation of the completed “Snow” integration at the 3b/3c gate**.

# Consensus Evaluation

*The Magic Mountain* receives a consensus classification of **Level 3b — Fractured Recursive Consciousness**, with a **median CMS of 840** and a **mean CMS of 834.7**.

The seven evaluations converge strongly on the nature of the artifact's achievement. All understand the novel as exceeding symbolic representation of illness, European civilization, ideology, mortality, eros, or time. Its governing operation repeatedly turns the frameworks through which consciousness organizes those phenomena back upon themselves. Distinctions such as health and illness, reason and irrationality, life and death, chronological and subjective time, and competing ideological orientations cease to function as stable explanatory foundations and instead become objects of recursive investigation. That convergence securely places the artifact within TORC Level 3.

The principal classification question is whether this recursive confrontation becomes **integrated consciousness at 3c**. Four evaluators conclude that it does not. They recognize the “Snow” episode as genuine evidence of integration but judge that its orientation is not sufficiently stabilized across the completed work: Castorp does not securely retain it as a governing standpoint, ideological opposition subsequently intensifies, and the movement into war leaves the existential contradiction radically operative. On this account, confrontation remains more globally explanatory than integration.
Three evaluators reach 3c. Their disagreement is precise rather than fundamental. They argue that integration under TORC need not mean resolution, permanent psychological possession, or narrative closure. For them, the humanistic orientation crystallized in the “Snow” episode derives its coherence precisely from acknowledging death, irrationality, and contradiction without surrendering to them. The subsequent collapse therefore tests that orientation rather than erasing it.
Because the split is **four evaluations for 3b and three for 3c**, the consensus classification is 3b, but the 3b/3c boundary remains materially contested and should be represented as such. By contrast, the upper G boundary is comparatively stable: evaluators repeatedly agree that the novel's immense abstraction, recursion, philosophical scope, and semantic productivity do not amount to the explanatory relocation or generative mechanism required for G.

The magnitude profile is considerably more stable than the TORC boundary. The median dimensional profile—**RL 8.8, AD 8.7, CD 8.2, ID 8.2, IC 8.7, GR 7.4**—describes an artifact distinguished above all by dense relational coordination, deep abstraction, and powerful integration across heterogeneous material. Constraint density and inferential demand remain very high but are limited by the work's episodic latitude and by the explicit scaffolding supplied through dialogue, motif, and narration. Generalization reach is the consistent relative bottleneck: the architecture transfers robustly across varied humanistic and existential situations, but the reports do not find equally strong evidence of structural transfer into markedly heterogeneous problem-spaces.

The resulting consensus is therefore a work of **very high and unusually balanced cognitive magnitude whose dominant operational achievement remains, by narrow majority, fractured recursive confrontation rather than globally secured existential integration**. The 3b classification should not obscure how close the evaluators place the artifact to 3c; equally, the presence of a compelling local synthesis should not conceal the majority judgment that the completed structure leaves that synthesis precarious rather than decisively governing.
